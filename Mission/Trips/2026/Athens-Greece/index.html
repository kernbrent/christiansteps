Serving Together in Athens, Greece

A team of twelve people from Texas, Colorado, and Wisconsin traveled to Athens to serve beside local ministries, build relationships across cultures, and care for people experiencing homelessness, displacement, and exploitation.

The team

Twelve people, one shared purpose

Five team members came from Texas, five from Wisconsin, and two from Colorado. The group represented different ages, occupations, churches, and faith traditions. Morning devotionals, evening debriefs, shared meals, long walks, and ministry assignments helped twelve individuals become a team.

Arriving with purpose

Beginning at Mars Hill

After gathering in Athens, the team traveled to Mars Hill. Looking toward the Acropolis and the city where Paul spoke in Acts 17, the group shared a devotional and communion at sunset. That first evening established the posture for the days ahead: observe carefully, listen before acting, and remember that God was already at work long before the team arrived.

Malakasa

Soccer beyond language

The team traveled by train to a Sudanese refugee encampment near Sfendali. Everyone played soccer in socks so differences in access to shoes would not determine who could participate. Five hours of drills, games, food, and conversation showed how play can cross language barriers and create shared joy.

Albanian Church

Worship, play, and fellowship

Time with members of the Porta Albanian Church included games with children and teenagers, pizza in the park, worship, communion, fellowship, and a shared meal. These were not projects to complete; they were opportunities to build friendships and enjoy one another’s company.

Athens streets

Homeless outreach

Guided by Arturo and local homeless ministry, teams prepared and distributed sandwiches, juice boxes, candy bars, and other supplies. More importantly, they stopped to talk, listen, remember names, return to see people again, and confront the loneliness and vulnerability experienced by people living on the streets.

Glocal Cafe

Welcoming refugees

At Glocal Cafe, the team helped serve more than 150 refugees, distributed clothing, painted a room, found a patio umbrella for rooftop shade, played games, visited a park, and shared conversations with people from many countries. The work combined hospitality, practical help, and patient presence.

New Start Ministries

Prayer in a place of exploitation

Dimitrius introduced the team to New Start’s work addressing prostitution and human trafficking. Prayer walks through neighborhoods containing hundreds of brothels made the scale of exploitation visible. The experience called the team to pray for women being exploited, for the men participating in the system, and for those working faithfully toward freedom and restoration.

Ancient Corinth

Scripture made tangible

A guided visit to Corinth connected biblical history with the stones and streets of the ancient city. The Bema, the Erastus inscription, and the commercial crossroads Paul knew helped the team understand the New Testament in its historical setting and reflect on a God who moves toward people in sacrificial love.

Formation within the team

Devotion, debriefing, and honest community

Service days were framed by Scripture, prayer, and honest reflection. The team considered compassion, humility, courage, unity, surrender, and the call to carry the experience home. Debriefs made room for laughter, tears, fatigue, disagreement, encouragement, and growth. Learning to support another person’s gifts—and sometimes setting aside personal ambitions—became part of the mission itself.

Physical and emotional fatigue were real. Long walks, heat, blisters, heavy stories, and the limits of what a short-term team could accomplish forced everyone to depend on one another. The goal was never to “fix” Athens. It was to serve faithfully, strengthen local work, and allow God to reshape the people who came to help.

Carrying it home

The mission continued after Athens

A day on Aegina gave the team time to rest, celebrate an engagement, affirm one another, and prepare for many different journeys home. The final challenge was not simply to remember the trip, but to live differently because of it—to notice people at home, move toward those who are marginalized, serve without superiority, and remain attentive to God’s work in everyday life.

The trip was fully funded through gifts to Christian Steps Ministries. Those gifts provided travel, lodging, ministry outreach, meals, supplies, and the opportunity for twelve people to join local partners in Athens. The original fundraising letter is preserved as part of the trip record.
